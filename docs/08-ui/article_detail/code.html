Understanding Neuroplasticity During Rehabilitation

Dr. Sarah Jenkins, PT, DPT
Published Oct 24, 2023 • 5 min read

Neuroplasticity, or brain plasticity, is the brain's remarkable ability to reorganize itself by forming new neural connections throughout life. In the context of rehabilitation, understanding this concept is crucial because it forms the biological basis for relearning lost skills and adapting to new physical realities.

When an injury occurs, whether it's neurological like a stroke, or musculoskeletal, the brain's existing pathways can be disrupted. However, the brain is not static; it is highly dynamic. With targeted, repetitive exercises and focused therapy, you are actively encouraging your brain to rewire.

Key Principles for Recovery

To maximize the benefits of neuroplasticity during your rehabilitation journey, consider these fundamental principles:

  • Use it or lose it: Neural circuits that are not actively engaged in task performance begin to degrade.
  • Use it and improve it: Training that drives a specific brain function can lead to an enhancement of that function.
  • Specificity matters: The nature of the training dictates the nature of the plasticity. You must practice the exact skills you want to improve.
  • Repetition is essential: Induction of plasticity requires sufficient repetition. This is why consistency in your exercise routine is vital.
  • Intensity matters: The training must be sufficiently intense to stimulate neural changes, while remaining safe and manageable.

The Role of Sleep and Stress

Physical exercise is only one part of the equation. The actual process of neural consolidation—where temporary connections become permanent—often occurs during deep sleep. A consistent sleep schedule is a non-negotiable part of your recovery plan.

Furthermore, chronic stress can inhibit neuroplasticity. The release of cortisol over prolonged periods can negatively impact the brain's ability to learn and adapt. Incorporating mindfulness, deep breathing, and restorative activities into your daily routine is just as important as your physical exercises.
